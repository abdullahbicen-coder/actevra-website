03 / ROBOTICS & INSPECTION

Robotic Assembly & Inspection Line

Assembly automation combining robots, machine vision, ultrasonic welding, fixture management and poka-yoke functions under central PLC coordination in a multi-station production line.

PROJECT EXAMPLE
01 / CONTEXT & CHALLENGE

Establish reliable process synchronization across multiple stations and robots

The line consisted of numerous interconnected assembly stations and different process devices. Robot entry/exit, part pick-and-place states, ultrasonic welding steps, fixture/jig conditions and camera inspections had to progress without conflicting with table movements. Setup and production information also had to be exchanged with the higher-level production system.

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

Station-based handshake and validation logic

Production states such as ready, process complete, waiting for material, fault and permission to rotate were defined for each station. Separate PLC–robot communication structures handled reference, station, welding, material-release and process-complete signals for multiple robots. Vision and inspection results were connected to poka-yoke logic, while setup, downtime, timing and batch information were synchronized with the higher-level production system.

03 / ENGINEERING SCOPE

Main engineering areas covered in the project.

  • Multi-station automatic assembly line
  • PLC handshaking with multiple robots
  • Ultrasonic welding sequences
  • Machine vision / inspection
  • Poka-yoke and incorrect-process prevention
  • Fixture / jig and setup management
  • Operator HMI and station diagnostics
  • Higher-level production-system data exchange

05 / OUTCOME

The robotic assembly line was engineered around automotive production traceability requirements rather than operating as an isolated automation cell. Product identity was maintained throughout the process while robot operations, ultrasonic welding, vision inspection, fixture status and poka-yoke results were associated with the relevant product record.

Product labeling provided the link between the physical part and its digital production history. Production orders and required product information were exchanged with the worldwide MES infrastructure, while production results and traceability data were returned to the higher-level system.

Robotic assembly, quality inspection, labeling and MES communication were therefore combined into a single traceability chain, enabling production and inspection records associated with a manufactured product to be reviewed retrospectively.
