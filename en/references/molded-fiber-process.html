04 / CUSTOM MACHINE DEVELOPMENT

Custom-Built Process Machine Development

Automation engineering supporting the development of a production machine tailored to the manufacturer's own process as an alternative to purchasing an off-the-shelf machine.

OutcomeLower investment cost

An in-house production machine tailored to the manufacturer’s process.

info@actevra.com.tr
01 / CONTEXT & CHALLENGE

Develop an in-house production machine instead of purchasing an off-the-shelf solution

The manufacturer wanted to develop the required process machine around its own production needs rather than purchase a ready-made system. The objective was to reduce investment cost while retaining the freedom to shape the machine around product and process requirements. Because the machine itself was still under development, the automation had to support changing process conditions and different what-if scenarios instead of merely executing a fixed production cycle.

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

Automation designed as part of the machine-development process

The control software was structured to adapt as process conditions evolved during machine development. Servo position, torque and motion parameters, together with spray, vacuum, tank, pump, valve and temperature processes, could be adjusted independently while remaining coordinated within the production cycle. Different motion sequences, process timings and operating scenarios discovered during build and commissioning were implemented in the automation, allowing the mechanical design, process and control system to evolve together.

03 / ENGINEERING SCOPE

Main engineering areas covered in the project.

  • Multi-axis servo motion
  • Position, offset and torque parameters
  • Tank-level and pump control
  • Valve and flow sequences
  • Spray / vacuum timing logic
  • Multi-zone temperature control
  • PackML machine-state management
  • TwinSAFE and safety I/O integration
04 / OUTCOME

The project resulted in a custom-purpose process machine developed around the manufacturer's own production requirements. Building the machine in-house provided a lower-investment alternative to purchasing an off-the-shelf machine.

The automation system was used not only to control the finished machine, but as an engineering tool throughout its development. Different process conditions and what-if scenarios encountered during build and commissioning could be implemented in software, supporting the joint development of the mechanical design, process and control system.
