02 / MOTION & FORMING

Servo Press & Forming Line

A multi-axis production machine synchronizing high-force pressing with servo motion, force feedback, temperature control and product-transfer mechanisms.

PROJECT EXAMPLE
01 / CONTEXT & CHALLENGE

Manage press force, position and product transfer safely within the same cycle

Press motion alone was not sufficient. Product feeding, chain transfer, ejector mechanisms, adjustable upper structure, discharge conveyors and temperature-controlled process zones had to operate in the correct sequence. In addition to continuously monitoring press force and the position of both shafts, the objective was to increase the existing production rate of 32 presses per minute without changing the mechanical system.

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

Motion and process control combined in one cycle model

Dual servo press axes were synchronized using absolute positioning, press duration and recipe-dependent stroke values. Force-sensor feedback monitored pressing force while shaft/encoder deviations were incorporated into fault scenarios. Chain transfer, upper feeding, ejector blade, upper-body adjustment, separators, stacker and discharge structures were coordinated with the main production cycle. The servo-drive motion profiles were then analyzed for cycle-time improvement. Acceleration, deceleration and jerk parameters were retuned within the mechanical motion limits to reduce time lost during axis movements.

03 / ENGINEERING SCOPE

Main engineering areas covered in the project.

  • Dual servo press-axis control
  • Force and shaft-position monitoring
  • Product recipes: stroke, dwell time, temperature and product dimensions
  • Ejector mechanism and product-transfer sequences
  • Adjustable upper structure / mechanical positioning
  • Multi-zone temperature control
  • TwinSAFE and EtherCAT integration
  • Energy consumption and machine diagnostics

05 / OUTCOME

Optimizing the servo motion profiles increased press capacity from 32 to 40 presses per minute. This delivered a 25% increase in cycle capacity on the same machine without requiring a mechanical capacity upgrade, while theoretical cycle time per press fell from approximately 1.875 seconds to 1.5 seconds, a 20% reduction. The performance improvement was achieved within an integrated control architecture covering dual-servo synchronization, force and position monitoring, and product-transfer sequencing.
